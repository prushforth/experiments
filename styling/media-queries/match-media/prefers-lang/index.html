Choosing map content by prefers-lang, with matchMedia()

The same three basemaps as the declarative version — English, French, and OpenStreetMap for everyone else — but all three are declared unchecked and hidden, and a pair of (prefers-lang: …) matchers decides which one goes on the map.

MapML.js (Leaflet)

The reference implementation. Because the choice is made after the elements are connected, all three layers exist for a moment before one wins.

MapML-MapLibre

Same handler, same three candidates. The language preference comes from navigator.languages, so it is the browser's answer, not the page's guess.

MapML-OpenLayers

Two matchers are needed rather than one, because the interesting outcome is a three-way choice and a matcher only answers yes or no.

The three outcomes

Changing your preferred language

  • Chrome: chrome://settings/languages — add languages in order of preference and set the first one to a language and locale, then restart.
  • Firefox: about:preferences → Language → Choose…, beside “Choose your preferred language for displaying pages”.
  • Edge: edge://settings/languages — add languages and restart for the setting to take effect.

The handler earns something the attribute cannot express: a real else. The declarative version has to write the fallback out as (not (prefers-lang: en)) and (not (prefers-lang: fr)), and that negation has to be kept in step by hand every time a language is added. Against that, the attribute never leaves three layers momentarily undecided, and never fetches the two that lose.

prefers-lang is under review in both forms: HTTP and HTML already choose a representation by language with hreflang and content negotiation, and a language is arguably not a property of the medium at all.
