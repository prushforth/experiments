TMS maps

The Canadian Forest Service's Fire Weather Index, published by GeoServer as an OSGeo Tile Map Service, over the Canada Base Map from a WMS. A TMS tile set counts its rows up from the bottom of the map rather than down from the top, and <map-link tms rel="tile"> is all an author needs to say so. The same extent carries a <map-link rel="query">, so clicking the map asks the service's WMS for the index at that point.

All three implementations draw every cell and answer the query. Where they differ is in what they ask the two services for, and each service shows it:

  • The basemap — once the map is wider than about 480px, its view reaches past 180° west. MapML.js and MapML-MapLibre ask the WMS for the whole view, and the Canada Base Map service then leaves most of the mainland out: it comes back white. MapML-OpenLayers clips its request to the projection's bounds, and gets the provinces and territories drawn. A frame's Open full page link shows it.
  • The TMS tiles — the service covers only the north-western quadrant of the world. MapML.js and MapML-OpenLayers ask for the four tiles in it; MapML-MapLibre also asks for lower-zoom tiles and the neighbours of the view, and the service answers those outside its coverage with 404s.
