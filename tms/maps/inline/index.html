Experiments

TMS maps, declared inline

The Fire Weather Index as GeoServer publishes it, a Tile Map Service, over the Canada Base Map from a WMS: one <map-layer> with an extent for each. A TMS counts its tile rows up from the bottom of the map, and the bare tms attribute on <map-link rel="tile"> is the whole declaration of it. Click the map to query the index at that point.

MapML.js (Leaflet)

The reference implementation of the tms attribute: the row from the template is counted from the bottom of the tile matrix instead of the top, and nothing else about the template changes.

Its WMS request covers the whole view. When the view reaches past 180° west, the Canada Base Map service leaves most of the mainland white.

MapML-MapLibre

The tms attribute becomes MapLibre's own tms tile scheme on the raster source.

MapLibre also asks for lower-zoom tiles and the tiles around the view. The service covers only the north-western quadrant of the world and answers the rest with 404s; nothing is missing from the map, but the console fills up.

MapML-OpenLayers

Clips the WMS request to the projection's bounds, so the basemap is drawn whole even when the view is wider than the world, which is where the other two frames' basemaps differ from this one.

What to try

  • Each map starts static, so scrolling past it does not zoom it. Release it with the lock button to pan and zoom.
  • Click a coloured area: the <map-link rel="query"> sends a WMS GetFeatureInfo request to the same GeoServer layer and shows its FWI value.
  • Open the layer control: the TMS extent is listed with its own opacity slider, while the basemap extent is hidden and only the layer as a whole can be turned off.
  • Use a frame's Open full page link: once the map is wider than about 480px its view reaches past 180° west, and only MapML-OpenLayers keeps the basemap's mainland.

The fire weather tiles come from the Canadian Wildland Fire Information System's GeoServer, whose TileMap description declares the bottom-left origin the tms attribute stands for.
